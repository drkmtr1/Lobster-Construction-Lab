## 5. Exact joint two-layer criterion

Start with the same centered shift of A by G on both coordinates. Let O=F+G be the upper outer block's start. In each coordinate the vacant indices are in [0,G−1] or [O,O+G−1]. Every active low displacement has magnitude at most h−1. A low row crossing blocks would have absolute displacement at least F+1≥2h, so it is impossible. Thus it must be in the same block and project to v=u+3−k. At high offset 1≤k≤h−2, the sum of the two actual new coordinates must be F+2G−1−k. Two lower coordinates sum at most 2G−2, which is too small by F+1−k>0. Two upper coordinates sum at least 2(F+G), which exceeds the required sum by F+1+k>0. A high row therefore crosses blocks and projects to u+v=G−1−k. These endpoint arguments use the actual h and old F≥2h−1, without a bound on G relative to F.

A complete fresh assignment projects to a bipartite multigraph on an input and output copy of [0,G−1], with degree 2 at each vertex. Every edge retains its actual host tag and separate identity. Impose the exact fresh row counts at each host, which are 2 for a grown host and zero for a nongrown host in the binary domain. More generally impose any specified nonnegative counts totaling 2G. At each projected input, the two incident edges must use different input-block bits. At each output their output-block bits must differ. If the input bit is x_e, the output is x_e XOR t_e, for type t_e equal to 0 at low and 1 at high. The constraints on consecutive edges e,f are
```text
at an input:   x_e XOR x_f = 1
at an output:  x_e XOR x_f = 1 XOR t_e XOR t_f.
```
Each degree-two component is a cycle of even length, allowing two parallel edges as a cycle of length two. Propagate from a chosen edge bit around a cycle. The sum modulo 2 of all right-side constants is the parity of the number of its high edges. The base 1 contributions sum to zero, and each edge's t_e appears once at its output. Hence return consistency holds exactly for cycles of even high parity. The equations are then satisfied by the propagated bits with two possible initial choices per cycle. Parallel edges with opposite types fail, while parallel edges with the same type lift to distinct actual rows. There are no loops since input and output are disjoint copies. G=0 gives the empty graph and the empty lift.

Lift a consistent graph by adding O times the selected bit to each projected coordinate. The projected edge equations recover the actual affine equations by the preceding block calculation. Degree and bit constraints give one use of each actual input and weight coordinate in each outer block. Aggregate host counts, existing terminal incidences and the skeleton are then exact by Sections 1 and 2. Thus the criterion is iff for a **supplied graph** and yields an exact feasibility formulation for the centered architecture after imposing degree and quota equations. It gives no independent source of a graph by itself. A supplied B yields parallel copies at each edge of its matching, so outer addition is a special case. Section 6 proves a strict separation from single-packet duplication even for binary counts.

There is also a closure constraint on repeated centered transports. If we start at a fixed A, later centered insertions translate its rows by the sum of their widths in both coordinates. All final fresh rows therefore lie in the two outer blocks for the total width and must satisfy the same joint criterion. This inclusion does not assert that any supplied joint graph splits into independently duplicated packets.
