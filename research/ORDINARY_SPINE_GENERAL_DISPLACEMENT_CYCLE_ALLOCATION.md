# D135 — General displacement-cycle allocation in the D134 cut state

## Exact state and necessary moments

Fix an exact integer p≥1 and h=2p+1. Use a supplied [D134](ORDINARY_SPINE_PIECEWISE_CUT_SPECTRUM_TRANSITION.md) return state with n edges, oriented cut c, exact actual crossing spectrum `[d,n]`, and actual last spine vertex at zero or n as recorded by the polarity. In the oriented coordinates G the last vertex is zero. Retain the original graph, labels, cut membership, full graceful inventories and ordered spine. The state obeys `n−2c=h−1` and `d−c=p+1`.

Prescribe independent exact positive integer quotas r_j at the physical centers j=0 through 2p. Put `Q=sum(r_j)`, `N=Q−r_p` and `k=(h+Q+1)/2`. Require even Q, Q≥h+1 and n≥h+Q. Thus k is an exact integer, positive and at most Q. The bands `[0,k−1]` and `[k,Q−1]` have lengths k and Q−k, using empty intervals when necessary.

The terminal with gap index i would have weight `d+i+p−j` at physical host j. To use the whole terminal difference inventory and give D134's crossing return at the prescribed split, seek a permutation π of `[0,Q−1]` that maps each band to itself and uses displacement p−j exactly r_j times. Summing `π(i)−i` over all positions gives zero by bijectivity, or
```
sum_j (p−j) r_j = 0
sum_j j r_j = pQ.
```
Summation over either invariant band separately also gives zero. This is necessary in the specified placement, including its host labels. At p=1 only the two outer hosts contribute, with offsets +1 and −1, so their quotas must be equal. The construction below shows that the same moment allows genuinely nonsymmetric quotas for every p≥2.

## Bounded displacement walk and exact cycle extraction

Assume the global moment vanishes. Start with N distinct consumable tokens, consisting of r_j copies of p−j for each j≠p. They are nonzero exact integers of absolute value at most p and sum to zero. Copies of the same value may be identified by their occurrence order. Start the walk at x=0. If x≤0 choose an unused positive token, and if x>0 choose an unused negative token, always the smallest available absolute value of that sign. As the walk is the cumulative sum of consumed steps, the sum of unused tokens is −x. If x<0 this sum is positive and at least one positive token is available. If x>0 a negative token is available by the opposite inequality. At x=0 with tokens remaining their total is zero and none are zero, so both signs are available.

Every position lies in `[1−p,p]`. The start lies there. From an integer x in `[1−p,0]`, a positive token gives a position in `[2−p,p]`. From x in `[1,p]`, a negative token gives a position in `[1−p,p−1]`. This includes p=1, whose allowed positions are 0 and 1. Every step consumes one token. After exactly N consumptions the position is zero by the remaining-sum identity.

Maintain a stack of distinct positions of the retained path, from zero to the current walk position, with the token of each intervening edge. When a new position is absent from the stack, append its edge and position. When the next position equals an existing stack position, record the directed suffix from the prior occurrence to the old end followed by the new closing edge to that occurrence. Remove the corresponding suffix edges and the positions strictly above the repeated one, retaining it as the end. This records a cycle with distinct vertices until its closing repetition. A nonzero step cannot close a loop of length one. Its edge tokens consist exactly of the removed suffix tokens and the fresh token, with no other token changed. Thus after each step all consumed tokens are partitioned between the recorded cycles and the retained stack edges. Extracting a cycle also has zero net displacement, consistent with the actual current position. At the end x=0, so distinct stack positions force the stack to be just its initial zero. The extracted cycles contain every consumed token once, in an extraction order determined by the stated rule.

## Independent allocation blocks and two-band packing

For a recorded cycle on L distinct coordinates u_1 through u_L, with the outgoing edge from u_L returning to u_1, let `w=max(u_i)−min(u_i)+1`. Translating its coordinates by minus the minimum embeds the cycle in a width-w interval with no change in any directed displacement. At each cycle vertex the physical host is j=p minus its outgoing displacement. At any unused coordinate in the interval choose j=p, the zero displacement, so that its image is itself. The cycle and fixed points permute the entire reserved interval. The block uses L nonzero tokens and w−L zero tokens. The walk bound gives `2≤L≤w≤2p`. With t cycles and total width W, token conservation gives `t≤N/2` and `N≤W≤2pt≤pN`. These are sufficient resource estimates for this decomposition.

In extraction order choose the longest initial list of complete blocks of cumulative width T≤k. Pack it consecutively starting at zero. Pack the remaining blocks consecutively starting at k, requiring `W−T≤Q−k`. Fill all other coordinates with fixed points. Both sets of blocks are disjoint and stay wholly within their assigned bands, so they give a permutation of each band. All nonzero displacements retain their original multiplicities. The zero count is `(W−N)+(Q−W)=Q−N=r_p`, including fixed points inside and outside blocks. Each gap label is allocated once at the specified physical host. This is a sufficient test for the selected extraction and order. A failed packing test does not imply that another decomposition, order or permutation fails.

A uniform independent sufficient capacity is `Q≥pN+2p−1`. If all blocks fit below k, the empty upper tail needs no space, including if its band is empty. Otherwise the next width obeys `0≤k−T<w_next≤2p`. The quantities are integers, hence `k−T≤2p−1`. With W≤pN,
```
W−T = W−k+(k−T) ≤ pN−k+2p−1 ≤ Q−k.
```
Every equality is allowed at the weak capacity inequalities, including `k−T=2p−1` if it occurs. In the no-next-block case no bound on leftover lower space is needed. This corollary gives a conservative sufficient total. The actual width test can succeed without it. D134's symmetric construction retains its separate sufficient domain and need not pass this more conservative test.

## Nonsymmetric independent supply

For every fixed exact p≥2 let symmetric positive nonmiddle quotas b_ell be given at physical p−ell and p+ell for ell=1 through p. Increase r_(p−1) by two, introducing two +1 displacements, and r_(p+2) by one, introducing one −2. Both hosts exist for p≥2. Their added total is `2(+1)+(−2)=0`, so the moment still vanishes. The ±1 and ±2 quota pairs are now unequal. All nonmiddle quotas stay positive and `N=2 sum(b_ell)+3` is odd. Choose r_p independently among sufficiently large positive odd integers so that `Q=N+r_p` is even and meets both `Q≥h+1` and `Q≥pN+2p−1`. For each such vector require n≥h+Q. Thus every fixed odd h≥5 has an infinite admitted nonsymmetric quota domain, without limiting the cycle test to those perturbations.

For each particular symbolic admissible vector, D134's independent initializer applies to a supplied maximum-frontier seed. Its parameter c_0 can be arbitrarily large in its allowed parity class. The first returned size is `N_seed+h+Q_0+1=4c_0+4p+2` with `N_seed=3c_0+p−1`, `Q_0=c_0+p+1`. Choose that size at least h+Q. This meets the new size bound at the same returned invariant and does not depend on constructing a numerical initializer. The nonvacuity claim is conditional on the already supplied maximum-frontier seed, as in D134.

## Exact section, crossing inventory and closure

Attach w at the actual old frontier v. Place the h fresh centers j at w in their given physical order and attach exactly r_j fresh named terminals to j, retaining all old names and incidences. These additions give h+Q+1 new edges and the same number of new vertices. Adding each as a pendant in order preserves the tree. The witnessing spine extends by w, old distances remain bounded by two and new centers and terminals are respectively one and two edges from it.

Use D134's oriented placement with `m=n+h+Q+1`, `F(old)=h+G(old)+Q indicator(G(old)≥c)`, `F(w)=m`, center j labeled j and gap labels `x_i=h+c+i`. Give x_i to physical j=`p+i−π(i)`, the unique host with displacement π(i)−i. All such j are in `[0,2p]` because the block displacement multiset is the prescribed one. Scanning i in increasing order and keeping one counter per host emits the exact terminal names in that host's index order. The actual absolute edge difference is
```
x_i−j = h+c−p+π(i) = d+π(i).
```
Every gap label is distinct and above the center labels. Both old bands, the gap, center labels and w fill `[0,m]` exactly once. Noncrossing old edges use `[1,d−1]`, terminals `[d,d+Q−1]`, old crossing edges `[d+Q,n+Q]`, and the connector and attachments `[n+Q+1,m]`. The connector is m−h=n+Q+1 and the attachments are m−j. Each band comes from distinct actual named edges and together gives the complete weight bijection.

Choose `c_out=h+c+k` in the uncomplemented output. Old membership persists there in oriented coordinates, by the bounds on k, and every center sits below c_out while w is at or above it. The new terminal edges cross this cut exactly when their physical label coordinate i≥k. Because π preserves the bands, crossing terminal weights give `[d+k,d+Q−1]` and noncrossing terminals give `[d,d+k−1]`. Adding old crossing weights and the connector and attachments gives the complete crossing interval `[d+k,m]`. At actual low input use F, which returns maximum frontier. At actual high input use m−F, which returns zero. Its actual old shifts are 1 and Q+1 on the low and high sides of the corresponding actual cut, as follows from `G=n−g` and `m=n+h+Q+1`. The low-input shifts are h and h+Q. The actual return cut is c_out for low input and m+1−c_out for high input. Both operations preserve actual side membership, since the two complements in the high-input case exchange sides twice at the corresponding cuts.

The actual new frontier is the last named w. The next oriented cut is `c_new=m+1−c_out=c+k`, since `2k=h+Q+1=n+Q+2−2c`. The new minimum crossing weight is `d_new=d+k`. Thus `n_new=m`, `n_new−2c_new=h−1` and `d_new−c_new=p+1`. The bounds on c and d persist by D134's cut calculation. This is the same state with opposite actual frontier polarity. No alpha separation is needed.

For a finite declared list at fixed h choose either the new allocation when it passes or a D134 symmetric allocation in its existing domain. Every chosen allocation satisfies the same two band invariants, so the exact transition above holds at each admitted size, by induction. The empty list is allowed. The deterministic cycle decomposition, split and capacity depend only on quotas and p at the returned invariant. Hence an admitted nonsymmetric vector remains admitted at every repetition as n increases. The process terminates for every finite declared list.

## Final alphabet and complexity

For each boundary i let C_i be its actual cut and let S_i sum all later gaps, A_i all later actual lower shifts. Actual cut membership persists, so the later composite on that boundary alphabet adds A_i below C_i and A_i+S_i at or above C_i. The local actual embedding followed by the next composite equals the previous composite pointwise on the old vertices, since both per-side shift sums agree. Emit each physical vertex once using the composite at the boundary where it is first supplied. The already emitted endpoint of the next actual connector retains exactly its final label. The used label bands and their exact residual complement in the final alphabet are
```
used      [A_i,A_i+C_i−1] ⊔ [A_i+C_i+S_i,A_i+n_i+S_i]
remaining [0,A_i−1] ⊔ [A_i+C_i,A_i+C_i+S_i−1] ⊔ [A_i+n_i+S_i+1,n_final].
```
Every actual noncrossing weight stays unchanged by later shifts and every crossing weight gains S_i. Thus the used and residual weights are
```
used      [1,d_i−1] ⊔ [d_i+S_i,n_i+S_i]
remaining [d_i,d_i+S_i−1] ⊔ [n_i+S_i+1,n_final].
```
Specifically, for a step from n,d to m with Q,k and later gap sum S, the previously used bands are `[1,d−1]` and `[d+Q+S,n+Q+S]`. New noncrossing terminals fill `[d,d+k−1]`, new crossing terminals `[d+k+S,d+Q−1+S]`, and the new connector and attachments `[n+Q+1+S,m+S]`. The separate terminal difference bands proved for π are exactly the required dependencies of D134's proof. Their disjoint union with the old bands is `[1,d+k−1] ⊔ [d+k+S,m+S]`. The fresh labels mapped into the final alphabet are disjoint from the old labels by the local label partition and injectivity of the composite on its two ordered bands. The only incidence between the completed vertices and the unassigned suffix is the next original spine connector, if there is another section. Every completed section has all its center and terminal incidences supplied. Both residual inventories are empty at the final boundary.

An indexed algorithm may use two count arrays over absolute sizes 1..p and one monotonically increasing cursor per sign to choose the smallest available token. These cursors together skip O(p) exhausted entries, since a count never increases. A position-to-stack-index array of size 2p gives constant indexed membership and insertion operations and permits clearing the entries of popped positions. Traversing, copying and removing extracted suffixes costs O(N) in total because removed edge occurrences are not visited again and each consumed token is recorded at most once as a closing or stack edge. Compute extrema while recording cycles and scan their widths once for the longest prefix. The decomposition and packing decision use O(p+N) indexed operations and O(p+N) storage. On acceptance, initialize fixed points on both bands and place each cycle at its reserved base, then emit in gap-index order. This costs O(Q+N+p) indexed operations, including maintaining host counters, with O(Q+p+N) storage if materializing the permutation. This bounds indexed operations, not arbitrary-precision bit cost. Sizes, cuts and polarities can be planned forward and shift sums backward as in D134. For an admitted list the sum of section allocation and emission costs is linear in the final number of new vertices and edges because Q≥h and N≤Q at every section. The supplied prefix can be read and emitted once.

## Controls and scope

From a zero-moment vector with r_p≥2, moving one terminal count from the middle to p−1 preserves total Q and all positivity but raises the moment by one, proving a placement obstruction regardless of available capacity. For the split control, take an intact directed cycle translated into a disjoint reserved position range that meets both sides of k, with available room, vacating its original coordinates and leaving spare positions fixed. Such translation keeps the global cycle permutation and every edge displacement. A directed cycle meeting both bands must exit each into the other at least once. Thus an upper source has a lower image, and the upper coordinate set no longer equals its image under π. These sources determine the actual crossing incidences, so the crossing spectrum fails even though the full terminal spectrum and quotas persist. This is a symbolic mutation with no numerical permutation or extension. The capacity control is the maximal-prefix slack proof above, including its limiting weak inequality.

D134's earlier symmetric transpositions and cut-split condition remain a separate constructive supplier on the same state. [R226](../references/upstream/mdl/N226_R226_two_host_completion.md#n226-closeout) and [R232](../references/upstream/mdl/N232_R232_joint_allocation.md) address supplied reserved two-host palettes and their distinct joint allocation conditions. Here the coordinate cycles construct compatible palettes for the fixed placement from the quota moment and capacity. D131–D133 and retained composition theorems keep their respective embedding and topology scopes. No arbitrary-quota classification, broad impossibility from packing failure, or universal coverage is asserted.
