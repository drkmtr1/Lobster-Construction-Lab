# D206 boundary-root joint-block construction

Task LCL-ORDINARY-SPINE-BOUNDARY-ROOT-JOINT-BLOCK-001. The substitution below joins a supplied ordinary graceful prefix and a supplied alpha block at their one actual common spine vertex. The explicit block extends the exact A, B and C inputs by two original sections simultaneously and admits finite repetition.

## 1. Shared boundary and exact resources

Let the named prefix T have n edges and independent graceful labels f on [0,n]. Let its physical last spine vertex v have f(v)=0 in the minimum normalization. Let the independently supplied block H have k edges and alpha labeling g on [0,k], with cut A+1 and g(v)=A, where 0≤A<k. Each H edge has one endpoint with g≤A and one with g≥A+1. The named exit w is distinct from v. The operands share exactly v. For a lobster assertion, require that the original witnessed spine paths concatenate at v, with all original incidences preserved. Union at one vertex of two trees is a tree. Every vertex remains within distance two of the concatenated spine if it was within distance two of its own witness. There is no extra edge or replacement branch.

Place T at F(x)=A+f(x). Place a fresh vertex with g<A at g and a fresh vertex with g>A at n+g. The shared name is already installed by T at A. Because g is a bijection, it is the only name at the local label A. The three disjoint consecutive label intervals are [0,A−1], [A,A+n], and [A+n+1,n+k]. Their sizes are A, n+1, and k−A. Old edges keep their exact differences [1,n] by translation. For any original H edge, write its local endpoint values x≤A<y. The combined endpoint values are x and n+y, including x=A for edges at the shared entry. The actual difference is n+(y−x). Since the local alpha labeling is graceful, those weights exhaust [n+1,n+k]. The edge sets are disjoint, so the combined labels are graceful on the exact original.

At physical cut C=A+n+1 all old edges are below the cut, and every H edge crosses. Thus crossing is [n+1,n+k] and noncrossing is [1,n]. Write P(m,c,D) for a normalized minimum-frontier labeling with cut c, crossing spectrum [D,m] and noncrossing spectrum [1,D−1]. If g(w)=0, the output frontier is 0 and the state is P(n+k,n+A+1,n+1). If g(w)=k, the output frontier is n+k and reflection at n+k puts its cut at k−A, giving P(n+k,k−A,n+1). The graceful union and spectra still hold when the distinct exit is internal, with iteration through that exit requiring a separate extreme-frontier premise.

For an actual incoming maximum frontier, let p(x) denote the physical old labels, with p(v)=n. Normalize by f=n−p, construct F, then complement the entire output at n+k. In these physical coordinates the old label is p(x)+k−A, the shared entry is n+k−A, and fresh lower-local and upper-local labels are respectively n+k−g and k−g. The labels used by those fresh classes are [n+k−A+1,n+k] and [0,k−A−1]. The old interval is [k−A,n+k−A]. At physical cut k−A all old vertices lie on the upper side. The same difference spectra hold because complement preserves subtraction magnitude and reverses both sides. For g(w)=0 the physical exit is now maximum, and for g(w)=k it is minimum. These are the same two normalized states as in the minimum-input construction.

All cut and threshold parameters are proper for k≥1 and n≥0. The interval [0,A−1] is empty at A=0. Then g(w)=0 is unavailable for a distinct w, but a g(w)=k exit is allowed. At A=k−1 the fresh upper class has one member. For n=0 the prefix is a singleton with no edge weights, [A,A+n]={A}, and the formula recovers the local alpha labeling or its complement. The two orientation descriptions can be chosen freely for that singleton.

## 2. Finite supplied lists and final emission

For each supplied block i let its edge count be k_i, boundary label A_i, and preceding prefix size n_i=n+sum of k_j for j<i. Its exact named entry is the previous frontier. Each exit used for the next attachment is extreme, and the union retains the given spine witness at each attachment. Birth is at stage 0 for the starting prefix, and at the unique introducing block for every other name. At each later stage j an already born physical label gains t_j=A_j if the input at j is minimum and t_j=k_j−A_j if it is maximum, by the polarity formulas above. The polarity is maintained if the local exit label is zero and reverses if it is k_j. An internal exit can be used at the final step without a normalized extreme state.

Give every name its exact birth label from the corresponding physical formula, then add the sum of later t_j. The shared root of a new block is an earlier name and is excluded from its new births. This emits exactly one final label per original name. Induction gives bijection at every block boundary and identifies the summed translations with the actual stepwise labels. Subtracting the two final endpoint labels gives the actual difference of each original edge, even when its endpoints have different birth stages. Once an edge is installed later translations leave its weight fixed. The original prefix contributes [1,n], and block i contributes [n_i+1,n_i+k_i]. These intervals partition the final weight alphabet. The inter-section connectors are actual spine edges of the supplied blocks. In minimum physical input the first connector from v to an upper local neighbor of label b has actual coordinates (A_i,n_i+b). Its complement is (n_i+k_i−A_i,k_i−b). The later common shift adds to both entries. The other spine edges are computed from their own birth maps.

Before each block the eventual final labels for old names occupy one translated interval, and the exact remaining label and weight inventories are the complements of installed names and actual installed incidences in the final alphabets. This definition applies also to original section boundaries inside a block using the final plan. An inner section boundary carries no requirement of a graceful interface in a compressed alphabet. At the last cut all older blocks and the original prefix lie entirely on the old side. The crossing band is exactly the final block's interval. The finite index induction terminates after the supplied list length. An empty list leaves the original input and its supplied interface as they were.
