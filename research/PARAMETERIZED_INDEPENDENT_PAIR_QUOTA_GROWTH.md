### Focused unequal augmentation attempt and obstruction

One possible way to remove a disparity between pair quotas is to replace the size-eight augmentation in §3 by a common translation of only p positions, increase n to n+p, return the same anchors at their required positions, and allocate only the leftover resources to chosen pairs. For p=1,2 an existing lower residual input 2−p necessarily shifts to the new reserved lower input 2, so this particular uniform translation fails. For p≥3 the same endpoint comparison as in §3 gives exact fresh lower sets [0,p−1] excluding 2, plus p+2, and [0,p−1] excluding 1, plus p+1. At n=4 the old lower residual weight set is empty and n+p≥7, so the new lower weight anchor is available and the new residual set is exactly that second fresh set. Both upper fresh sets have offsets [−1,p−2] relative to F+2n+p. L₇ and L₈ already have their replacement rows, so the new rows can use only the new roles. In these local coordinates highs have sum p−1−k.

The isolated lower input p+2 cannot go to an upper weight at a high: the required upper offset is −3−k<−1. At any of L₁,…,L₄ its lower weight is p+5−k, and the only available such value is p+1 at L₄. Therefore every augmentation within this common-translation template necessarily spends a fresh row at L₄. In particular it cannot independently increase any one of the other paired offsets. Even increasing **only pair 4** by p at each of its two roles fails. The fresh upper weight p−2 cannot be filled at L₄ without upper input p−1, or at H₄ without lower input −3. Both are absent. The obstruction survives changing which numerical offset receives a single allowed pair increment, and does not depend on the old packet's detailed rows. It still assumes this old-row transport inside the packet and the same anchors. It places no exclusion on a joint reallocation of old and fresh packet rows, other states, or the graceful original.

The p=4 fresh palettes also rule out adding two at each of two distinct paired offsets. Pair 4 must be one of them. Its H₄ rows are forced at LU (0,−1) and UL (−1,0), its only two possible crossing rows, and L₄:(6,5) still holds. Since the fresh block has four highs and balanced lower counts, it needs exactly two LU, two UL and two lower lows. After those forced rows, one further lower low must occur. If the other pair has offset 1, lower weight 3 can only be filled by L₁ from lower 1, since H₁ would need the already used upper input −1. Then lower input 3 has no legal H₁ crossing, since it needs the used upper weight −1. At offset 2, lower weight 3 has no eligible lower low or UL row. At offset 3, lower weight 3 forces L₃ from lower 3 and then lower input 1 has no legal H₃ crossing, as its required upper weight −1 is already used. Thus every nonzero paired even increment vector of total p=4 fails this same uniform-translation template.

Thus the proved alphabet-wide result for independently variable four pairs consists of the full homogeneous subfamily, along with uniform increases by two in every component of any separately supplied returning seed. One may subtract a common even amount, such as min(q_k)−1, from a requested odd tuple and use a separately supplied positive for that smaller tuple in the forward direction. There is no converse from a failure at the smaller tuple. The unequal tuples are not classified by D230, and no role-order obstruction proved here excludes them from the general returning geometry.
