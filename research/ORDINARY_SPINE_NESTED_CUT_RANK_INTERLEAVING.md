## 1. Nested-cut theorem and reversible ranks

Use an actual graceful \(n\)-edge prefix with labels \([0,n]\), \(n\ge1\). For the unique edge of difference \(d\) and endpoints \(x_d<y_d\), define its crossed integer cuts by \(I_d=[x_d+1,y_d]\). The interval has exactly \(d\) elements. If every cut \(r\in[1,n]\) has crossing differences \([D_r,n]\), then membership \(r\in I_d\) is equivalent to \(D_r\le d\), implying \(I_1\subset I_2\subset\cdots\subset I_n\). With \(I_0=\varnothing\) each successive difference \(I_d\setminus I_{d-1}\) is a single cut \(r(d)\). Hence the \(D_r\)'s are a permutation of \([1,n]\). Conversely, if the intervals are nested by inclusion, their cardinalities order them by rank. Every proper cut is in \(I_n=[1,n]\), and its first membership identifies its contiguous upper spectrum. These are equivalent conditions.

An increasing integer embedding into \([0,M]\) has the unique form \(E(x)=A+x+\sum_{r\le x}g_r\), with \(A=E(0)\ge0\), \(g_r=E(r)-E(r-1)-1\ge0\), and \(B=M-E(n)\ge0\). For an actual edge of old rank \(d\), subtraction yields
\[
 W_d=d+\sum_{r\in I_d}g_r=d+\sum_{D_r\le d}g_r,\qquad
 W_d-W_{d-1}=1+g_{r(d)},\quad W_0=0.
\]
Thus old differences remain strictly increasing in their old rank. Conversely, take any increasing positive integer sequence \(W_1<\cdots<W_n\le M\), and \(0\le A\le M-W_n\). Define the nonnegative widths \(g_{r(d)}=W_d-W_{d-1}-1\). Summation gives \(E(n)=A+W_n\), \(B=M-W_n-A\), and the same subtraction proves exact reconstruction of every old weight. This is a bijection of embeddings with legal sequence-offset pairs.

The used labels are the named values \(E(0),\ldots,E(n)\). Their complementary lower and upper exterior bands are \([0,A-1]\), \([M-B+1,M]\), with \(A+B=M-W_n\) after counting their cells. Between consecutive old labels the exact fresh band for cut \(r\) is \([E(r-1)+1,E(r)-1]\). The used edge weights are \(W_1,\ldots,W_n\). The exact fresh weight complement is \([W_n+1,M]\) and the bands \([W_{d-1}+1,W_d-1]\). The two bands paired by \(d=D_r\) both have size \(g_r\), including empty intervals. These are exact disjoint partitions obtained by consecutive endpoints. Actual original fresh incidences must supply the numerical resource complements.

In the D196 terminal-free step with \(a\) original children, temporary old labels are uniformly translated by \(a\) into \([a,a+n]\). Every old cut interval translates by \(a\). Thus arbitrary actual finite terminal-free runs from these independently supplied seeds retain the nested property, with every original parent and port intact.

Under any increasing embedding old intervals remain nested as endpoint intervals, even after inserting gaps. For a completed graceful extension, write \(J_u\) for the final cut interval of the actual edge of difference \(u\), and let \(J_0=\varnothing,\ J_{M+1}=[1,M]\) for boundary tests. The output is nested exactly when each chain of fresh intervals between consecutive old ranks is nested in its own increasing weight order and bounded by the adjacent old intervals, using these artificial bounds only at the first and last block. Equivalently, in full difference order the lower interval endpoints weakly decrease and the upper endpoints weakly increase. Concatenating and restricting the chain prove the two directions. The test is about the actual complete graph. In fact if all intervals nest, each next edge extends one of the two endpoint labels by one and connects the new vertex to the retained opposite endpoint. A vertex can have as nonleaf neighbors only its neighbor when introduced and the last new neighbor introduced before its endpoint is displaced. Thus the remaining induced graph after deleting leaves has maximum degree two. The odd and even full controls have a receiving vertex with four nonleaf neighbors and fail this necessary property for all-cut closure. Section 5 also gives a direct interval failure for the multi-gap composition. Nesting of an incoming prefix therefore imposes no automatic closure at a successful return.
